What I work on

  • Relativistic environments

    Massive black holes rarely sit in empty space. I work out how discs and scalar clouds around spinning black holes respond to a small body, the spiral density waves it launches, and the forces that push back on its orbit, all in the Kerr geometry.

    Spiral density waves in Kerr (2026); Perturbations to the environment in Kerr (PRL, 2025)

  • 1PA waveforms

    LISA will follow extreme-mass-ratio inspirals for tens of thousands of orbits, so their waveforms need first post-adiabatic (1PA) accuracy. I contribute to the second-order self-force programme, and to precision modelling of the environmental effects that enter the waveform at 1PA.

    Kerr metric perturbations for second-order self-force (2026)

  • Strong-field gravity

    Exact results close to the horizon: plunging orbits in Kerr, charged particles around magnetic black holes, and how light and waves behave in the strong field.

    Plunging geodesics in Kerr (CQG, 2023); Magnetic black holes (PRD, 2023); Lensing and wave optics (PRD, 2025); Relativistic aerodynamics: the black hole Magnus effect (PRD, 2024); A relativistic third law (in prep.)

Hej, I'm Conor

I'm a postdoc in Clément Bonnerot's tidal disruption event group, in the School of Physics and Astronomy and the Institute for Gravitational Wave Astronomy at the University of Birmingham. There I'm bringing relativistic, strong-field perturbative methods into the modelling of tidal disruption events and quasi-periodic eruptions. More broadly, I'm interested in the interplay between matter and the motion of gravitating bodies in strong-field gravity.

I'm from Poulacapple East, on the border of Kilkenny and Tipperary in Ireland. I studied applied and computational mathematics at University College Dublin, did Part III at DAMTP in Cambridge, and then did my PhD in the Center of Gravity (formerly the Strong Gravity group) at the Niels Bohr Institute in Copenhagen, where Maarten van de Meent and Vitor Cardoso were my supervisors. When I'm not doing physics I'm usually at rugby, squeezing an Anglo concertina, or explaining why Crowley should be wearing 10.
